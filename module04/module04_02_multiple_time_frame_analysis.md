# Module 4, Video 2: Multiple Time Frame Analysis (Top-Down Analysis)

Hello and welcome to video number two, part of Module 4. We're still tackling multiple time frame analysis. One thing I'd like to introduce, which is very much part of the analysis we've been conducting, and which I want you to get comfortable with, is **top-down analysis** — basically, going from higher time frames to lower time frames. The idea is to look at an asset — a stock, an FX pair, whatever you're trading — and understand the context in which it's trading, historically speaking, and where we think it might be going.

The important thing is to look not at just one time frame, but multiple time frames. Some people have a tendency to trade off a single time unit — a five-minute bar chart, a one-minute bar chart, tick charts — and I think they lose perspective. That's the most important thing: you have to keep a global, time-based perspective of where we are at any given moment. So the idea is to look at weekly and daily charts first, then go down the line to hourly charts, and fine-tune with 15-minute and 5-minute units of time.

## Why Multiple Time Frames

The main purpose of this exercise is to find convergence among all these time units. The more convergence we get from the long term to the medium term to the short term, the higher the probability of success on our trade — provided, obviously, that we trade on the same side as the prevailing long-term trend.

**Long-term trend:** the longer units of time — weekly and daily — help us determine the longer-term trend of the market: where it's been, what it's been doing, and where we expect it to go.

**Intermediate units of time:** I split this between stocks and non-stock instruments (FX, or commodities like gold or silver that trade around the clock). For stocks, my intermediate unit is one hour; for FX, it's four hours. Why? Simply because stocks trade regular hours, while FX is open more or less 24/7 (apart from weekends), so I adjust the intermediate unit accordingly.

**Shorter units of time:** 15 minutes, 5 minutes, or even 1 minute. These are the units I use in multiple time frame analysis to find trade setups and fine-tune trade entries on every single trade.

Remember what we discussed about identifying price clusters on our charts — areas of price where multiple indicators converge (moving averages, Fibonacci retracements, multiple horizontal lines clustering into a resistance or support zone). These are very much part of our multiple time frame analysis too, helping us know where we are in context: yes, the long-term trend (from weekly/daily) might be up or down, but where are we relative to a main, primary, monthly or weekly support or resistance? Should we be aggressive going long or short on the instrument we're looking at? That's why these price cluster areas — along with Donchian channels, etc., covered in previous modules — are important to draw on our charts, and we'll see many examples together now.

The idea is to choose the correct side of the trade based on the prevailing long-term trend (weekly/daily), then use counter-trend waves on shorter units (15-minute or 5-minute) to fine-tune the entry — for instance, playing a breakout, or waiting for a pullback after a breakout. This is where the shorter units of time — 5 minutes, 15 minutes, 1 minute — come into play.

Basically, we're putting all the pieces of the puzzle together — building blocks, module after module, leading up to the exact methodology we'll apply. By coinciding this convergence across multiple time frames — for trend, for support and resistance levels — while waiting for a proper setup that leads to an impulsive move, we get what we're really after: instruments with real momentum. We don't want choppy or rangy markets, because they can stay stuck for a long, long time, tying up our capital in inefficient trades. So we look for instruments showing a clear trend, on the back of a proper setup likely to lead to an impulsive move — and when we get that full convergence of elements, our probability of success on every single trade increases tremendously.

And again, I'll reiterate: this is such an important part of your methodology, your active investing — we need to stick to strict risk and money management. When we do that, in addition to the convergence of all time frames and all the signals in our entries and setups, we can trade and invest with no stress at all.

## Example One: HCA Holdings

Let's bring up the charting software. I have my screen split into four time units for stocks: weekly, daily, hourly, and 15-minute.

On the **weekly** chart, HCA has been in a very strong uptrend since the beginning of 2018 — up and up and up, with a recent small correction and consolidation around the 20-week moving average. That was an area of interest for us — a potential zone to look for buys if our methodology's signals dictate it.

On the **daily**, the trend mirrors the weekly — also a strong uptrend. At this stage, both weekly and daily long-term trend indicators point to a very strong uptrend, so this is the side of the market I want to trade — I want to be long. So I'd be looking for opportunities to get back long on shorter-term units.

Now, at this stage the market had dipped through the 50-day MA, never a great omen, and we have to be careful. But often we also see some kind of consolidation or bounce between the 50 and 200 MAs. To confirm the right timing, we go to the **hourly** chart: this shows the correction the market had gone through — a very choppy, rangy market, with the moving average trending down and price below it. This is not where I want to buy — the short-term trend indicates the stock wants to go lower, and I want confirmation of convergence between price moving higher and the moving average turning as well on the intermediate (hourly) time unit. I want maximum convergence across all time units — weekly, daily, hourly, and the short-term (15-minute, 5-minute) units.

At this stage, I had marked an area corresponding to the 20-week MA. I didn't know for sure the market would react there, but it was an area of interest — and indeed, the market bounced off the 20-week MA and started trending back up. Now, moving back above the moving average on the hourly, we get the right convergence across time units, and I feel a lot more comfortable that I'm in line with the trend, even the intermediate one.

Dropping to the **15-minute** chart: the market was trading comfortably below the 10, 20, 50, and 200 MAs — a time to be careful, as the market was signaling further downside. It made a first attempt to bounce off the support area, then found big resistance at the 200-period MA on the 15-minute chart — which usually marks the stepping stone determining whether the market goes up or down. It acted as resistance, dipped back down, then broke back up — at which point price showed full convergence with the one-hour, daily, and weekly charts, and I became comfortable getting back in, provided the reward justified the risk.

Looking at price action: the stock had been consolidating for a few days in a roughly $4 range, between about $133 (support) and $137 (resistance). On the breakout day, we got a gap up — the market opened around $136.75, already through some of the Donchian resistance levels. Going to the 5-minute chart to fine-tune the entry: playing a simple breakout above roughly $130 (without waiting for a pullback), the stop would go below the range, around $134 — a $4 stop. My minimum acceptable risk-reward would be 1:1.

Looking back at the daily chart with an old high around $141: buying the breakout at $138 with a target of $141 is only $3 of reward for $4 of risk — not a great trade on its own. But if you believe the sector overall was doing well (healthcare, sector indices strong), you might still take it, placing the stop less aggressively just below the breakout level (around $135-136) rather than at $134. That reframes it as risking about $3 to make about $3 up to the $141 old high — a much more sensible 1:1 risk-reward trade.

This illustrates the process: establish the long-term trend on weeklies/dailies, check the intermediate trend on the hourly, then fine-tune on the 15-minute — looking for convergence, for a breakout of a known resistance or consolidation level, and confirmation via volume.

## Example Two: UBNT-Type Stock

Same process. I won't show the sector index, but it was also one of the top performers, so the trend was well established.

On the **weekly**: an obvious uptrend, visible through the Donchian "staircase" levels and the convergence of moving averages, with price staying above the 200-week MA except for one gap area. Price found support at an important Donchian support level and between the 50 and 200 MAs, as discussed before. Recently, the stock had been consolidating and finding support around $83-86, also corresponding to the 20-week MA — I marked this as a potential area of interest.

On the **daily**: same picture — a clear long-term trend with the 10, 20, and 50 MAs parallel, and the 200 MA lagging but still comfortably below price. The same consolidation area shows up here — the market wasn't doing much for quite a few days, which isn't what we want; we'd rather see the stock start moving, otherwise we're tying up capital for no reason beyond "yes, this is a support area."

On the **hourly** (intermediate trend): at this stage the market looked uncertain, clearly trending down intermediate-term. I didn't want to buy into that area — even though it was a support zone on the weekly, I lacked full convergence between weekly, daily, and hourly. I wanted to wait for more convergence. Interest picked up once the market started trading up again and showed some convergence — though even then, nothing exciting was happening yet; moving averages were flat, indicating a choppy market.

Dropping to the **15-minute** chart: still nothing exciting — price trading comfortably within a range-bound Donchian channel, flat moving averages — until, gradually, the short-term trend started moving up, with price above the 10, 20, 50, and 200 MAs, back in sync with the established long-term trend.

That's when the stock gapped up on good earnings, with the sector doing very well over the preceding weeks and months. It opened around $99.50, up from a prior close of $94.67 — quite a big gap, opening well above the consolidation area and all our established 15-minute resistance levels. This gave full convergence across weekly, daily, hourly, and 15-minute charts.

Where were we in context? Opening with a gap above the recent resistance and consolidation zone, but also opening very close to the stock's all-time high. Since we opened at $99.50, we'd need to clear the all-time high around $150 (approximate) to prove the market had the momentum for a big move up. With full trend convergence across all units, I was happy to buy this breakout — checking off all my parameters, trading a breakout above main resistance, with the next resistance around $101.50 (or the old high). Practically, I could either wait for the 15-minute opening range (which had already cleared comfortably above the old high) and buy on a new high on the second 15-minute bar, or wait 5 minutes minimum after the open. My stop would sit a few cents below the day's low, giving roughly a $4 risk on a stock with a strong long-term trend making a new all-time high on unusually large volume — odds strongly in my favor. It turned out to be a good trade.

## Example Three: A Stock Like "Z"

Same process again — I'll walk through the steps; later modules will cover the exact methodology in detail. Right now it's about screen time — training your eyes and brain, developing your own style.

On the **weekly**: a very strong uptrend, moving averages nicely parallel and rising, the Donchian "steps" going up, higher lows and higher highs established. Everything says the long-term trend is well established and up. Around a support level of about $83.60 (near the 20-week MA), the market corrected — and we thought this was a good place to look to get back onto the trend.

On the **daily**: same picture — strong, steep, parallel 10/20/50-day MAs, a very strong uptrend, with the market finding support around the Donchian levels and the 50-day MA multiple times in the past. This brought us to the marked area corresponding to the weekly 20-week MA level, around $82.35-$83.50 — an interesting zone to look for a re-entry if signals confirmed.

On the **hourly** (intermediate): at the point the market was consolidating and pulling back into this area, it was clearly choppy, with moving averages bunched close together and roughly flat, then the market moved down below the 200-hour MA, with moving averages converging downward and price trending below them — not a place to buy. Even though we know this area is a marked support zone on the weekly, and the long-term trend is up, the intermediate trend told us "not yet" — the intermediate trend was down even though the long-term trend was up.

Finally, as the market reached that dailies/weeklies support zone, it started bouncing — stabilizing, with moving averages flattening and starting to look for a signal to re-enter. Price then moved back up, with moving averages converging upward (VWAP, 10, 20, 50 all turning up), breaking back above the 200-hour MA, and the price opened with a gap above a resistance level around $89 that we had identified.

At this point, the intermediate trend converged with the established long-term trend from the dailies and weeklies, and I was comfortable getting back into the market.

On the **15-minute** chart: the consolidation area we'd flagged on the hourly showed good resistance levels around $89 — a level the market had been stuck under, that was finally broken through, giving full convergence across intermediate, short-term, and long-term trends.

The market opened with a gap above resistance on that day at $89. We can trade this two ways: wait for the opening 15-minute or 5-minute range (depending on strength/volume) and go long on a new high above that range (around $90.50), with a stop just below the day's low ($88.75) — about a $2 risk. Looking back at the dailies, the first real resistance above was around $96, and the all-time high was around $100 — so buying the breakout near $90.50 offered roughly $6-10 of potential reward against about $2 of risk — a very favorable setup given the full convergence, volume confirmation, and a supportive news catalyst.

Alternatively, you could wait for a pullback to the 20-period MA and enter around $90.30 (close to the same $90.50 area) as the market started curling back up. Either approach worked — the trade ended up being very strong, running all the way past the old all-time high before eventually correcting (money and risk management for that will be covered in a later module).

The point of this example, again: multiple time frame analysis lets us build a plan — confirm the long-term trend on dailies/weeklies, gauge our position relative to the all-time high or latest high, assess reward potential versus risk, then drop to the intermediate (hourly) and short-term (15-minute) charts to find the right setup and confirm convergence. Here: full convergence, a nice breakout above important resistance, back in line with the established long-term trend, and reward clearly outweighing risk — a good trade, with good probability, and it worked out well.

## Example Four: GBP/NZD (Forex)

Now let's look at an FX example: the New Zealand dollar against the British pound (GBP/NZD). I'm generally a big fan of GBP pairs — it's the currency of a major country, but quite volatile, so not the first pair I'd recommend to someone just starting out. Still, it's one I follow because it offers the volatility needed to make money.

On the **weekly**: the market had been in a downtrend over the past few years, especially since the Brexit vote, before finding support since 2017 and turning up a bit. Marking key resistance zones from the Donchian levels: one around 2.04 (strong resistance, later broken), and a bigger historical level around 2.10 — still far from where price was trading at the time.

The long-term weekly trend was up, but remembering our discussion of cluster zones — areas where resistance/support and Fibonacci levels converge — the market was entering the price zone between 2.00 and 2.04, definitely an area to watch carefully.

On the **daily**: a big resistance zone existed where the market had consolidated for many months, roughly between 1.89 and 1.98. The market first broke through this resistance (around 1.98), consolidated for a few days pulling back to find support on the 50-day MA, then broke back up, cleared the old high around 2.00, and ran all the way to 2.04 — entering the area we'd flagged on the weekly as a significant resistance zone requiring caution.

This is exactly the exercise of reading context: where are we trading relative to historical zones of resistance and support? Is it worth being aggressively long here, knowing that above about 1.985 there was still room to the 2.04 level? But once we entered the historically significant 2.00-2.04 resistance zone, would I want to be aggressively buying? No.

In that zone, price consolidated and started trading down — first through the 10-day MA, then the 20-day MA (never a good sign, especially after a consistent uptrend over recent weeks/months), and finally broke through the 50-day MA, finding some support around the old 1.98 resistance-turned-support level, before consolidating around 1.88.

At that point, price was comfortably trading below the moving averages — the long-term trend had temporarily flipped to a downtrend on the daily. Checking the weekly again: less obvious, but price had also started trading below the 10- and 20-week EMAs, so while one could argue the very long-term trend might still technically be up, real caution was warranted, especially coming down from such a major resistance zone. On the daily, price was still above the 200-day MA but now below the 10, 20, and 50-day MAs — a moment to be very careful, and at that stage I was definitely looking to go short.

For the intermediate unit on FX, I use the **4-hour** chart (rather than 1-hour, which I use for stocks) since FX trades 24/7 while US stocks only trade about 6.5 hours a day (9:30am to 4:00pm). In the 2.00-2.04 consolidation/resistance zone, the 4-hour chart showed price trading below its 50-period MA — never a good sign — and eventually breaking through, giving full downward convergence: the intermediate trend matched the daily trend. Coming down from a major resistance zone after a long uptrend meant there were plenty of trapped longs likely looking for the exit as the market kept falling — reinforcing the bearish signal below the 4-hour 50-MA, matching the daily.

Ways to go short on FX: you can play breakouts of a recent low, but fakeouts are almost the norm on FX (more so than stocks), so I tend to advise smaller position sizes on breakouts, and prefer waiting for a pullback/consolidation back toward the moving averages — in this case the 10/20 MAs, quite steep and parallel, matching the daily and intermediate trend. Watching for bounces against these moving averages gave repeated opportunities; when price moved far below the 10-period MA during acceleration, a consolidation and bounce back toward the 50-period MA typically followed, and those bounce zones were also good places to look for setups.

On the **15-minute** chart (the shorter time unit for fine-tuning): this showed the last leg of the move, corresponding to the same zone on the 4-hour chart — full convergence, with price trading below the 10/20/50/200 MAs, repeatedly consolidating and finding resistance at the 50-period MA on the 15-minute chart. The setup to watch for was a bounce against the 50 and (especially) the 200-period MA on the 15-minute, which tends to mark the ultimate short-term trend. We got full convergence: Donchian channels showing the downward staircase, moving averages parallel and steep (showing momentum), and the Dow Theory pattern of lower highs and lower lows fully confirmed. That's the kind of market where we get full convergence from long-term to intermediate to short-term trend — and that's how we want to trade it: short, certainly not long, at that stage.

## Example Five: USO (Crude Oil ETF)

As a last example, let's look at a commodity — technically an ETF that trades on the stock market called USO (United States Oil Fund), though I actually traded this particular move via CFDs tracking WTI crude oil futures on the ICE market. When trading commodities, I generally prefer futures or CFDs tracking futures contracts rather than ETFs like USO, because ETFs like this can gap since they follow US market hours, while the underlying commodity might move overnight without the liquidity you'd want if trading off-hours. Not a huge problem, just a personal preference.

On the **weekly**: after the 2008 credit crunch, crude oil crashed, found a bottom around 2016, and consolidated for a long time. USO itself (which doesn't track the crude oil price 1:1, and isn't something I normally trade) consolidated for a long time roughly between $8 and $12.50 throughout 2016 into late 2017. Then the market started moving up, with moving averages converging nicely, the Donchian channel showing the staircase pattern going up, and price trading comfortably above the 10, 20, and 50-week MAs — so we can say the market had been in an uptrend since around June/July 2017.

Now, the key resistance zone: around $15.61 to $16.20 on the weekly, an important level also bumping into the strong resistance historically represented by the 200-week MA. Entering that zone, this wasn't going to be easy to clear. Going back to the previous example's logic: do I want to get aggressive once we're above $15, breaking through the established old high at $15.28? No, I certainly wouldn't want to be aggressive there. Do I stay with the trend? Yes — but I'd be watching for the market to show some kind of reaction in that zone. The reaction wasn't a full engulfing pattern on the weekly, but there was a definite break down, with price trading below the 10- and 20-week MAs — never a good sign on the weekly, especially coming off such an important resistance zone.

On the **daily**: same story — reacting at the big weekly resistance zone, the market consolidated and broke down below the 10- and 20-day MAs, found some support at the 50-day MA, and bounced — but at that stage there was nothing more to do long-side; the market was already signaling a change of trend, even while still above the 200-day MA. Breaking below the 50-day MA required extreme caution — for me, the trend was changing, and the intermediate trend confirmed it.

On the **hourly** (for the ETF itself; I'd use 4-hour for the CFD): the same major weekly resistance zone showed price trading below the hourly 50-period MA — not a good sign — bouncing off the 200-period MA, then finding the hourly 50-period MA acting as resistance again, bumping against the $15.28 price level (the prior breakout level, now turned resistance). Coming down from a zone of strong historical resistance, after a long uptrend where everyone was positioned long, expecting the market to keep climbing, and seeing this shift on both the intermediate and daily trend — I knew the long-term trend was changing. This was the stage to become aggressive on the short side, with full convergence across the intermediate and daily trend, adding to shorts on any break of recent lows.

I actually filmed this particular trade over a few days, executed through CFDs on my FXCM platform rather than the USO ETF itself. Reviewing the footage (sped up): I took an initial short on the break of a 4-hour candle — timing was a bit off, since we were still trading around the daily 50-MA, a somewhat risky place to go short — but I stayed patient, my stop gave some flexibility, and the market eventually started moving my way. I added another short corresponding to a convergence of moving averages on the 15-minute chart and the 4-hour 50-MA, with my stop again giving some room, and the market took a bit of time before moving as expected. Eventually it started trending down and took out the recent 4-hour low — at that point I took some partial profits but also played the fresh breakdown, initiating a new short on the break of that 4-hour low, and the downtrend resumed. I took further profits with a target near the daily 200-MA, taking partials there; I waited a bit as the market stabilized around the 200-MA, then became impatient and covered the whole remaining position.
