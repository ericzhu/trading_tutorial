## The Three Core Strategies

The three core strategies are designed to cover the broad spectrum of market configurations:

1. **MRA — Major Reversal Warning strategy**: aims to catch the turning point of the market (picking the top or bottom).
2. **Breaking Loose strategy**: a breakout strategy.
3. **B2B — Back to Trend strategy**: used once a trend is established, for re-entering, accumulating, or staying in/out while continuing to trade within that trend.

### How the three strategies interact (example walkthroughs)

**Example 1 — Verastem, Inc. (daily chart):** Highlighted blue zones mark major monthly/weekly support and resistance areas. At these zones, the MRA strategy is used to call the bottom (or top) of the move. Once triggered, subsequent Donchian channel levels and prior resistance areas trigger the Breaking Loose (breakout) strategy, while pullbacks to major moving averages trigger the B2B (trend-following) strategy — carrying price back up to the next major resistance zone, where the MRA strategy is used again to call the top, and the cycle repeats.

**Example 2 — Gold SPDR ETF (weekly chart):** Same cyclical pattern — MRA triggers at a major resistance zone to call the top; Breaking Loose and B2B strategies then manage the position down to the next major support zone, where MRA is used again to call the bottom (turn).

**Summary:** MRA helps identify the major trend change; once that change is established, Breaking Loose and B2B add to the position and manage it within the new trend until the next MRA opportunity appears at the opposite extreme.

Not all three strategies suit every trading psychology — some traders will be comfortable with all three, others with just one or two. As emphasized in the psychology module, you must know yourself and find your niche strategy, since each requires a different psychological approach.

## The MRA (Major Reversal Warning) Strategy

### What it is
The MRA strategy tries to capture the inflection point / turnaround of the market, aiming to be among the first positioned when the market changes gear and direction.

- **Pros:** excellent risk-reward ratio when correct.
- **Cons:** lower win rate than the other strategies — roughly **50–60%** — because picking market tops/bottoms is extremely difficult, both technically and psychologically. It is a pure **contrarian** strategy, so it doesn't suit everyone.

Importantly, this is not about blindly fighting the main trend — it targets very specific price areas that show strong support/resistance based on price convergence criteria. Sometimes it takes multiple attempts before the market moves as expected. Beginners typically give up after one failed attempt; professionals will try again (and again) as long as the trade remains fully consistent with their system's rules and risk management. As always: **trade the market you're given, not the market you wish you had** — the market is always stronger than any individual trader.

The MRA may be the most difficult of the three core strategies, but it might also become your favorite setup if it matches your personality. Only time spent practicing each strategy will reveal which fits you best. Above all, every strategy must be applied strictly within the risk and money management constraints from Module 5 — the backbone of any trading strategy.

### MRA Strategy Rules

1. **Location:** Price must be trading within a major monthly (minimum weekly) area of resistance/support. The more convergence of technical levels (Donchian channels, moving averages, Fibonacci levels), the stronger the zone.
2. **MACD weekly divergence** (a plus, not mandatory): a weekly MACD divergence strengthens the case that a major reversal zone has been reached. Divergence analysis is generally kept to the weekly chart (daily at most) since these give the most meaningful reversal signals.
3. **Chart pattern confirmation:** look for a double top/bottom (or near double top/bottom), or a consolidation phase where Dow Theory is being challenged (no more higher-highs/higher-lows, or lower-lows/lower-highs).
4. **Trigger rules:**
   - Primary trigger: a break of the **daily EMA 20**.
   - **Exception A:** if the EMA 20 is far from current price, instead look for a daily engulfing candle pattern combined with a breakdown/breakout of recent daily lows/highs.
   - **Exception B:** if the EMA 10, 20, and 50 are very close together and roughly parallel, use a break of the **EMA 50** instead for more credibility.
5. **Stop loss:** placed above the recent high (short) or below the recent low (long), or at 2× ATR on the reference timeframe — somewhere in between these references.
6. **Check for nearby resistance/support** that could stall the expected move; if present, ensure the risk-reward ratio (entry vs. stop vs. that nearby level) is still at least 1.
7. **Extra confirmation to watch for:** when the EMA 10, 20, and 50 are all slowly turning, and especially when the EMA 10 crosses through the EMA 50 in the direction of the turn, the probability of a validated reversal increases substantially. This "full convergence of elements" produces the most desirable trade setups.

*Practical tip: print this rule list and keep it at your trading station like a pilot's checklist — screen time and repetition are what make it become natural.*

## Worked Examples of the MRA Strategy

### Example 1: USD/JPY (short, weekly chart)
- Major weekly resistance area established; double top confirmed.
- EMA 20 was far from price at the resistance zone → looked instead for an engulfing pattern + breakdown of recent daily lows (both present).
- Confirmed by price breaking below the EMA 50 on the 4-hour chart → short triggered.
- Stop: above recent 4-hour high, ≈2× ATR (4H) ≈ 60 pips.
- **TP1** (60 pips, RR≈1): 25% covered, stop moved to breakeven.
- **TP2** (160 pips): 50% covered at daily support near EMA 50 daily; remaining stop converted to a trailing stop (RTS), triggered by price crossing above the 4H EMA 20.
- **TP3** was never reached — the trailing stop was hit instead, closing the remaining 25% for 115 pips.
- **Result:** £410 net P&L, 334 pips total.

### Example 2: AUD/JPY (long, monthly/weekly chart)
- Monthly chart showed a triple bottom in a major monthly support zone; weekly bull MACD divergence forming (price making new lows, but MACD histogram/lines not confirming).
- Daily chart: a sharp spike down, choppy back-and-forth, then a break above the daily EMA 20 plus a break of recent daily highs → long triggered; confirmed by price above the 4H EMA 50.
- Stop: below the spike lows (excluding the news-driven wick), ≈2× ATR ≈ 300 pips.
- **TP1** (300 pips, RR≈1): 25% covered at daily resistance near EMA 100; stop moved to breakeven.
- **TP2** target was EMA 200 daily, but the trailing stop (below 4H EMA 20) triggered first, closing 75% of the remaining position for 317 pips.
- **Result:** £174, 517 pips total.

### Example 3: AUD/JPY (short, monthly chart)
- Monthly triple top plus monthly bear MACD divergence in a major monthly resistance zone.
- EMA 20 far from the high → engulfing daily pattern + breakdown of recent daily lows, confirmed by price below the 4H EMA 50 → short triggered.
- Stop: ≈1.5× daily ATR ≈ 130 pips above recent highs.
- **TP1** (186 pips, RR≈1.2): 25% covered near daily support/EMA 50 daily; stop to breakeven.
- **TP2** (314 pips): 50% covered near EMA 100 daily/prior consolidation support; trailing stop set below 4H EMA 20.
- **TP3** (482 pips): remaining 25% covered at EMA 200 daily/major weekly support zone.
- **Result:** £700, 982 pips total, overall RR multiple of ~5 — illustrating how letting a trailing stop run the last portion of a position can capture the largest share of profit ("the cherry on top").

### Example 4: Gold futures (short, monthly/weekly chart)
- Monthly triple top with monthly bear MACD divergence; weekly chart confirmed a marginal new high with MACD/histogram rolling over (weekly bear divergence).
- Daily EMA 20 breakdown; entered half the position on a pullback, half on the break of recent daily lows.
- Stop: above recent 4H high.
- **TP1** at EMA 50 daily (RR≈1): 25% covered, ~2,100 pips banked; stop to breakeven.
- **TP2** target zone between EMA 100/EMA 200 daily; instead, trailing stop (price above 4H EMA 20) triggered, closing the remaining balance for 426 pips.
- **Result:** modest but positive trade — "green is always good."

### Example 5: Gold futures (short, another instance)
- Similar setup: pseudo-double top at monthly resistance, EMA 20 far from price → engulfing pattern + break of recent daily low as trigger (4H EMA 50 confirmation not usable since price was already far below it).
- **TP1** near daily support/EMA 20 daily: 25% covered, ~1,700 pips; stop to breakeven.
- **TP2** target near EMA 100 daily (~$1,310/oz); trailing stop (4H EMA 20) triggered instead, closing 75% of the remainder for ~100 pips.
- **Result:** £180 — a reminder that trailing stops sometimes exit a bit early (price spiked further before collapsing), but consistency matters more than maximizing any single trade.

### Example 6: Industrial Select Sector SPDR / XLE-type ETF examples (stocks)
- Weekly/monthly resistance zone with double top and weekly bear MACD divergence → daily EMA 20 break + break of recent daily low triggers a short, confirmed nicely on the 60-minute chart where EMAs converge and curl downward together with price breaking below all of them — described as "the perfect configuration to focus all your attention on."
- **TP1** (25%) near daily support/EMA 100–200 confluence, RR≈1; stop to breakeven.
- **TP2** (50%) at next Donchian support level, since the move is parabolic — trailing stop set below the daily VWMA 10.
- **TP3** (25%, remainder) closed either at the next chart level or when the VWMA 10 trailing stop triggers.

### Example 7: Energy Select Sector SPDR (XLE), long trade
- Weekly bull divergence at major weekly support; daily EMA 10/20 convergence, break above EMA 50 daily plus break of recent daily highs → long triggered.
- Stop: ≈2× daily ATR (~$4) below recent daily low.
- **TP1** (25%, RR≈1) at first daily resistance; stop to breakeven.
- **TP2**: either 35% at the next technical resistance, or 75% closed via trailing stop / breakeven stop — in this case, price never reached the TP2 target and instead broke below the EMA 50 trailing stop, closing 75% of the remaining position.

### Example 8: XLE, two alternative short-entry scenarios
- **Scenario A:** short entered on an earlier EMA 50/daily-low breakdown; price chopped back up (fake breakout) before eventually reversing down to hit TP1 and TP2 — technically profitable but psychologically "excruciating" due to the long, choppy wait.
- **Scenario B:** short entered on a later, cleaner EMA 50/daily-low breakdown — TP1 and TP2 both hit relatively quickly, with much less waiting. This illustrates that entry timing/quality matters even within the same overall setup.

## Key Takeaways
- The MRA strategy is the hardest of the three to trade well: it demands patience, discipline, and a contrarian mindset, and may take several attempts before succeeding.
- When it works — entering within roughly 5–10% of the actual top/bottom — the risk-reward can be exceptional.
- All the risk, money, and position management rules from Module 5 apply throughout: forex examples mostly used the intraday position management guidelines, while stock/ETF examples used the daily (swing) position management guidelines — the choice should match your own trading style, available time, and personality (medium/swing trading is slower-paced; intraday trading is faster and more demanding).
