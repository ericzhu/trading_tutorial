# Module 7, Video 3: Screening Pre-Market Gappers and Intraday Movers

This is the final video of Module 7, taking a closer look at the last aspect of the morning preparation routine: reviewing the morning papers/gappers to see if anything interesting should be added to the A watch list.

## Recap

So far, Module 7 has covered how to spend the weekend building the watch list to follow for the coming week, as well as the daily morning routine steps. Step 12 of that morning routine was running the usual scanners to identify any interesting additions to the list — specifically, going through the overnight or pre-market movers, known as **"gappers."** They're called that because they open significantly higher or lower than the previous night's official close, leaving a visible gap on the price chart.

The process starts with checking **Benzinga** (a website for news and insights on pre-market movers), then running the **Trade Ideas** scanner for pre-market gappers.

## The "Less Is More" Principle

It's worth pausing to reiterate an important point — one every trader has experienced: on a hot market day, there's a temptation to overload the watch list so as not to miss out on any play. The irony is that this overloading is exactly what causes plays to be missed, because it's simply impossible to track every single stock on a bloated watch list throughout the trading day.

The advice: instead of loading up the watch list with a pile of tickers, focus on the **highest-probability setups**. Finding 4 or 5 solid plays for the day is plenty — in reality, all that's needed to profit on a given day is **one solid play**. Building massive watch lists only dilutes a trader's focus, which is why the weekend A-list is deliberately kept to about **10–15 stocks or fewer**.

Each morning before the open, pre-market activity is reviewed to narrow the list down even further, since the A-list might not actually offer a tradable signal on any given day. For example: the plan might be to buy AMD on a pullback into a support area, but pre-market action shows it opening with a gap up, already more overextended than expected — signaling that AMD may not play out as hoped, meaning it probably won't be traded that day. This is exactly why room is left to add one or two names from the day's gappers — allowing focus to stay on the most profitable plays rather than wasting time chasing every single move. **Less is more.**

## Criteria for Adding a Gapper to the Watch List

A gapper only deserves attention if it fulfills specific criteria that are crucial to the strategy:

1. **Trend convergence filters (green).** The Trade Ideas gapper scan ranks names from the biggest positive gappers down to the lowest, and includes custom filters such as "Ultimate Bull," "Bull Reversal," "Strong," etc. — these trend-convergence filters are the first level of screening. If green, it means the gap is moving in the **same direction as the main trend**, and the stock is considered a play. If not green, move on to the next candidate.
2. **Pre-market volume.** A decent minimum volume is needed even in the pre-market session for a stock to be considered — anything trading only a few thousand shares is a pass. A bare minimum of **100,000 shares** in pre-market volume is required to retain attention, and the higher the relative volume (RVOL), the better. (RVOL itself isn't available before the official open, but pre-market share activity gives a sense of how it compares to the average daily volume.)
3. **Size of the gap.** Anything above **3%** is acceptable to keep on the radar, but anything above **30%** tends to be passed over — as covered previously, gaps that large tend to already be too extended. If the setup still looks compelling with the right convergence, it may still make the watch list, but an entry would wait for a decent pullback or consolidation.
4. **News catalyst priority.** Priority is given to a gapper with a breaking news catalyst of some sort, over one moving for no apparent reason (also covered previously).

If a gapper fulfills these criteria, the **Opening Price Range (OPR)** strategy is applied — typically the 15-minute OPR, or at minimum the 5-minute OPR if the setup is particularly liked. (Refer back to the breakout strategy video for a refresher.)

## Intraday: The Biggest Gainers/Losers Scanner

Throughout the rest of the trading session, an eye is kept on the **biggest gainers and losers scanner**. Four things are prioritized here as well:

1. A **green** trend-convergence filter (same as above).
2. A **red** relative volume (RVOL) filter.
3. A **red** volume filter.
4. A **red** StockTwits relative activity filter.

The redder these last three filters appear, the better the stock looks as a potential play (RVOL and volume: generally, the more volume, the better). **StockTwits** — for those unfamiliar — is one of the most active websites for stock-related tweet/social activity. Trade Ideas tracks this activity for every stock and calculates, for any given hour, the **relative activity** of stock-related tweets on that particular day compared to the norm. For example, if Apple typically shows around a hundred tweets before 10:00, but shows 200–300 on a given day, the relative activity would be running at three times the usual level. This filter is valuable because it gives a read on social media attention — the higher, the better, since it signals that a particular name is drawing significant attention from a lot of people online.

## Closing Thoughts on Module 7

This concludes Module 7 on how to prepare trading sessions. As should now be clear, a large part of trading success relies on preparation and doing the work — the idea is to be prepared for battle and come into the day with a game plan.

Even with a slightly larger-than-usual number of names on the A-list, that doesn't mean all of them — or even any of them — will be traded. As mentioned before, some days nothing at all may get traded; the point is simply to be prepared for the potential opportunities they offer. The same goes for gappers: something interesting that fulfills all the criteria being looked for might show up, or it might not — and that's perfectly fine, because **not trading is also part of trading**. It's better to sit tight, wait, and focus on the best setups, because eventually they will turn up.

Patience, discipline, and method are the keys to trading success.
