# Module 6, Video 4: More MTF System Examples — Weekly/Daily/60-Minute Screens (TCMD, HQY)

This video continues applying the MTF conversion system with a different screen combination: **Screen #1 = Weekly**, **Screen #2 = Daily**, **Screen #3 = 60-minute**. Two stocks are used: Tactile Systems Technology (TCMD) and HealthEquity (HQY).

## Example 1: Tactile Systems Technology (TCMD)

### Screen #1 (Weekly) — Strategic Decision
Since 2017, the stock has been in a clean uptrend, trading comfortably above the 10/20/50 EMAs. After a strong run and a first correction, the market slowly recouped all the way back to the top of the consolidation, around the prior weekly high near $38. Based on this weekly context, the strategic decision is to **play the market long**.

### Using Fibonacci Extension for Target Zones
Since the stock had already broken into territory with no prior resistance (trading since August 2016 with no overhead supply), a **Fibonacci extension** (using the 50%–61.8%–100% levels, as introduced in Module 2) was used to estimate where the next resistance zone would likely appear — the box marked from this extension became the area used to plan TP2/TP3 targets.

### Screen #2 (Daily) — Trigger Point Options
On Screen #2, two standard trigger types are available in the direction of the main (up) trend:
1. A pullback to an EMA or support area combined with a Stochastic RSI oversold reading, or
2. A breakout — typically a 1, 3, 6, or 12-month high breakout (or a mini-consolidation breakout).

**First breakout (3–6 month high):** Price broke above the 3–6 month high level, but the daily Stochastic RSI was already overbought at the time, and the move had already run up quite a bit from the base of its range. Two choices were considered:
- Wait for a better pullback entry where Stochastic RSI is closer to oversold, or
- Use Screen #3 (60-minute) to fine-tune the breakout entry.

**Screen #3 (60-minute) breakout trade:** Entering about $0.10 above the breakout level with a stop just below the 60-minute consolidation (~$0.98, rounded to $1 — consistent with 2× the ~$0.45–0.50 average ATR on the 60-minute chart) gave a risk/reward ratio of about 1 for TP1. After TP1, the stop was moved to breakeven — the position was later stopped out at breakeven, but the TP1 profit was already banked.

**Alternative (Solution #2 — waiting for the daily pullback):** After the breakout, price pulled back to between the EMA 20 and 50, then curled back up above the EMA 10. Entry there, with a stop below the recent daily low (~$2, consistent with roughly 2× the ~$1–1.20 average daily ATR), gave a risk/reward ratio of about 1 for TP1 around 36.80. This is generally the safer of the two solutions, since the breakout entry was riskier (daily Stochastic RSI was already overbought and price had already run up substantially) — which is also why breakout entries are only taken at 50% of normal position size.

**Key principle:** Screen #1 = strategic screen (direction). Screen #2 = tactical screen (can also serve as the trigger/trade-setup screen on its own). Screen #3 is used only when it can meaningfully improve the trade setup — a tighter stop, better risk/reward, or bigger position size. If Screen #1 and Screen #2 alone already give a good setup, there's no need to complicate things with a third screen.

### Second Breakout (12-Month High)
A second, larger breakout occurred at the 12-month high level (around 37.81) — visibly bringing in far more participants (a noticeably larger green candle and volume vs. average).
- Entry: about $0.15–0.20 above the breakout level (~38).
- Stop: just below the 60-minute consolidation, about $1.20 (average 60-minute ATR was about $0.40–0.80, so this is a comfortable stop, possibly even tighter than necessary).
- TP1 risk/reward ratio: slightly above 1.
- As is standard for breakout trades, only 50% of the normal position size was taken here. The other 50% is normally added on a pullback to the breakout area, a pullback to an EMA (10/20/50), or a mini-consolidation — but in this case the market ran up almost 20–23% ($8–9) without offering any of those opportunities. Note, though, that a prior position from the first breakout/pullback trade was already in place, so overall exposure wasn't zero.

### Back on the Weekly — Managing the Position Further Out
After the 12-month breakout, the market rallied hard through the previously planned TP2/TP3/TP4 zones (some filled, some caught by trailing stops), then reached the Fibonacci-extension resistance zone identified earlier on the weekly chart — exactly where the market was expected to stall, which is what happened. Price then corrected slightly back toward the first weekly support level, the EMA 20.

### A Further B2B Re-Entry (Weekly Fibonacci + Daily Confirmation)
Using Fibonacci retracement from the recent low to the high of the move: the 38.2% retracement level lined up closely with the weekly EMA 20 — a logical first support zone. A separate Fibonacci extension (38.2%–61.8%) was used to mark the next resistance box above.

On the daily (Screen #2), price approached this support zone, which also coincided with a convergence between the EMA 50 and EMA 100, plus a Stochastic RSI oversold reading — a classic B2B counter-trend opportunity.
- Entry trigger: price closing back above the daily EMA 20.
- Stop: below the recent lows (~$4.20 — consistent with roughly 2× the ~$2 average daily ATR).
- TP1: just below the recent daily highs (also corresponding to the 1-month Donchian high) — filled the same day. Stop then moved to breakeven (~51.60).
- Further targets (TP2, TP3) were set inside the resistance box established earlier from the weekly Fibonacci extension.

**Overall result:** starting from the 3–6 month and 12-month high breakouts, through the pullback/B2B re-entry, the full sequence of trades using this MTF setup performed well — overall roughly 100% ($35) price appreciation was captured across the various entries.

## Example 2: HealthEquity Inc. (HQY)

Same screen setup: Screen #1 = Weekly, Screen #2 = Daily, Screen #3 = 60-minute.

### Screen #1 (Weekly) — Strategic Decision
The uptrend has been well confirmed: every time price pulled back to the weekly EMA 50 and bounced, it resumed trading above the 10/20/50 EMAs. Focus is placed on the point where the stock broke its 6-month high — **strategic decision: play long.**

### Screen #2 (Daily) — First Counter-Trend Setup
At the 6-month high breakout level (5537), price broke through, then abruptly crashed in one big down day all the way to the daily EMA 200, bounced, and then ripped back through the same breakout level again. Since Screen #2 is the tactical screen looking for counter-trend moves in the direction of the main uptrend, this crash-to-EMA-200-and-bounce, combined with an oversold Stochastic RSI, marked the area of interest.

### Screen #3 (60-Minute) — Fine-Tuned Entry
Zooming into the 60-minute chart around this same event: price ripped through the breakout level, then reversed hard, with EMA 10 crossing through EMA 20/50 down toward a support level, Stochastic RSI also oversold at this point. Waiting for price to prove itself by trading back above the EMA 10:
- A stop right at the recent low would have been about $4 — too wide.
- 60-minute average ATR was about $0.60, so 2× ATR ≈ $1.20 — a more reasonable stop, placed below the low of three candles prior.
- TP1: risk/reward ratio of 1.
- TP2: targeting a retest of the high made after the initial 6-month-high breakout.

**Second 60-minute entry (later re-test):** After the trend reaffirmed itself through the 6-month high, another entry point appeared where price on the 60-minute chart showed a temporary loss of full EMA convergence (price still above EMA 200 but not fully aligned above 10/20/50), with Stochastic RSI clearly oversold, followed by an attempt at the EMA 50, a bounce, and then EMA 10 crossing back above EMA 20/50 with price trading above all EMAs again.
- The market had corrected almost $9 (from ~$69 down to ~$59-60).
- Stop: initially too tight at the exact recent low; adjusted to roughly 2× the ~$1 average 60-minute ATR (~$2), placed below the recent low.
- TP1: risk/reward ratio of 1 — filled a few bars later; stop then moved to breakeven (~62.14), never challenged again.
- TP2: set near the prior high of the move (~$68) — reached more slowly; rather than waiting passively, a **trailing stop based on the daily chart** (or alternatively, manually raising the stop to each new higher low as the Donchian channel steps up) can be used to protect paper profits while still aiming for TP2.

**Overall takeaway:** the same MTF logic applies regardless of the exact screen combination (weekly/daily/60-minute here vs. daily/240-minute/60-minute or daily/60-minute/15-minute in earlier examples) — Screen #1 sets the strategic direction, Screen #2 identifies the counter-trend tactical re-entry zone (via B2B pullbacks or breakouts), and an optional Screen #3 is used only when it can meaningfully tighten the stop or improve the risk/reward of the entry.
